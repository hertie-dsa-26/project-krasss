Climate scenario methodology

Future climate values are generated by fitting a linear trend (slope per year) on all available historical data for the selected county. That trend is then scaled by a scenario multiplier and applied cumulatively from the 2023 baseline:

projected_value(year) = baseline_2023 + (slope × multiplier) × (year − 2023)

Slopes are enforced to be physically consistent — warming variables such as TAVG, TMAX, DT100 are constrained to increase, while cooling variables such as HTDD, DT32, EMNT are constrained to decrease. The four scenarios correspond to different multipliers applied to the historical trend:

Scenario SSP analogue Trend multiplier Effect
Low warming SSP1-2.6 ×0.5 Trends continue at half the historical rate
Middle-of-the-road SSP2-4.5 ×1.0 Trends continue at the historical rate
High warming SSP3-7.0 ×1.75 Trends accelerate moderately
Very high warming SSP5-8.5 ×2.5 Trends accelerate strongly

Non-climate features (demographic, socioeconomic) are held constant at their 2023 observed values. At least 3 years of historical data are required to fit a trend; if unavailable for a variable, its value is held constant at the 2023 baseline.

Known limitations
  • Predictions are generated by models trained on 2013–2022 county-level data; performance on future years may degrade as climate and demographic conditions shift beyond the training distribution.
  • The Physical Health model has a negative R² on the held-out test set, meaning it performs worse than a simple mean prediction — forecasts for this outcome should be interpreted with particular caution.
  • The Sleep model (XGBoost) shows range compression: predicted values vary less than actual values, so extreme high or low predictions may be underestimated.
  • Non-climate features are held constant at their last observed values. Real-world changes in demographics, income, or healthcare access are not captured.
  • County coverage is limited to the 731 counties present in the merged dataset. Many rural and smaller counties are not represented.
  • Confidence intervals are conformal prediction intervals calibrated on the 2023 test set and may not fully reflect uncertainty over a 10-year horizon.
  • Climate scenarios are illustrative projections and do not correspond directly to IPCC SSP pathways or any specific emissions trajectory.
